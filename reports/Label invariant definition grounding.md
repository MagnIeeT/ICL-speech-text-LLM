# Train speech LLMs to read definitions, not names

A training strategy that makes a speech-text LLM's class predictions depend only on the label definitions in the prompt, invariant to whether classes are written as original names, random symbols or permuted real names, and robust to how the definitions are worded, is **an unclaimed contribution as far as these searches reach, and a meaningful one**. Every ingredient has been published separately. Symbol tuning trains on arbitrary labels. GoLLIE masks class names behind guidelines. Sun et al. add a KL consistency loss across instruction paraphrases. MCQ debiasing work trains for consistency across option permutations. KAFT mixes agreeing and counterfactual contexts to get faithfulness without losing accuracy. But **no paper found trains an explicit consistency or invariance objective across label renamings with definitions held fixed**, no benchmark varies class definitions while holding labels fixed, and no speech or audio LLM work outside the user's own Interspeech 2025 paper (arXiv 2505.07731) manipulates label surface form during training. The knowledge-conflict literature also supplies a ready mechanism for the chapter 1 result. Context-parametric inversion explains why IT keeps only 25% of its predictions under a name swap. FaithfulRAG and VQA debiasing explain why Flip pays about 10 points on original labels. That turns chapter 2 from "another augmentation" into a principled answer to a known trade-off. The main conceptual risk is that strict invariance rules out using name priors, so original-label accuracy is capped at what the definitions alone support. The chapter needs to decide, and state, whether its goal is strict invariance or "follow definitions under conflict, exploit names under agreement". The novelty claim rests on searches of limited depth (roughly 15 tool calls per sub-area). Run one last Semantic Scholar and OpenReview sweep before submission, and watch the NTU Lee group closely.

## Label names dominate predictions unless training removes their payoff

The ICL literature already predicts the chapter 1 pattern. Wei et al. showed that only large models override semantic priors when shown flipped labels, that small models degrade under semantically unrelated labels, and that **instruction tuning strengthens reliance on label-name priors** (arXiv only, venue unverified) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03846)). Symbol tuning (EMNLP 2023) fine-tuned Flan-PaLM up to 540B on exemplars whose labels were replaced by arbitrary symbols. It improved unseen ICL tasks when instructions or meaningful labels were absent and restored flipped-label following that instruction tuning had removed. Its appendix also reports that **per-exemplar random symbols, with no consistent mapping, generally did worse than standard symbol tuning** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08298v2)). That is a direct warning for the Sym regime, where the definition is the only signal left. Symbol tuning's flipped-label performance was, on average, not significantly better than random ([Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/symbol-tuning-improves-in-context-learning-in-language-models/)). At the 1 to 12B scale of Qwen2-Audio's backbone, a November 2025 preprint found a **semantic override rate of exactly zero across 8 models and 8 tasks**. It concludes that overriding label semantics at this scale needs interventions beyond ICL ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21038)).

The prompt-sensitivity literature points the same way. Webson and Pavlick found that models learn as fast from irrelevant or misleading templates as from good ones, but that **performance depends more on the choice of target words (the verbalizer) than on the prompt text** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.167)). Verbalizer manipulation (Findings NAACL 2024) is the closest text-LLM analogue of the Flip condition. Labels are specified in the instruction as natural, neutral or flipped words, and **GPT-4 scores barely above random on the hardest unnatural verbalizer** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.233)). Mechanistically, "Label Words are Anchors" shows label-word tokens collecting demonstration information in shallow layers, which deep layers then read (from prior knowledge, unverified this session) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14160)). That suggests a diagnostic for chapter 2: check whether information flows through definition tokens instead of label tokens.

Inference-time calibration reduces label bias but does not ground decisions in definitions. Domain-context calibration found that GPT-J's prior shifts to 0.95 "hate" versus 0.05 "neutral" when shown random in-domain words, and reported up to 37% Macro-F1 gains from correcting it ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.783)). A 279-task study across 10 LLMs found that **scale, demonstrations and instruction tuning reduce but do not remove label bias, and calibration can hurt** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02743v1)). These methods assume stable label tokens. They do not apply cleanly to per-instance random symbols, and none conditions on definitions or guarantees invariance to renaming.

Description-driven systems are the closest structural precedents. Label-description training gave **17 to 19% absolute gains over zero-shot** and lower sensitivity to label-to-token mappings ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.853)). GoLLIE represents labels as classes with guideline docstrings and regularises with class-name masking (for example "Person" becomes LABEL_1), guideline paraphrasing, class dropout and candidate sampling. Its ablation matters here: **removing name masking dropped zero-shot F1 only from 55.3 to 54.6 (p = 0.10, not significant)**, while removing candidate sampling dropped it to 49.9 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03668v5)). Masking as plain augmentation buys little, which is the strongest literature argument that an explicit invariance objective is needed rather than more data mixing. D3ST replaces schema names with descriptions plus index picking to avoid memorisation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08904)); whether its indices are permuted per example is unverified. SGD-X provides five paraphrased schema variants and a schema-sensitivity metric, and shows **12 to 18% joint-goal-accuracy drops** for state-of-the-art dialogue state tracking ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.06800)). Together, D3ST and SGD-X are the most complete precedent pair for "descriptions instead of names, evaluated under paraphrase". They are also close to speech intent and slot tasks.

## The IT versus Flip trade-off is a known knowledge-conflict problem

The chapter 1 result maps almost exactly onto context-memory conflict. The "memory" here is the lexical meaning of the label token, not a world fact. The knowledge-conflict survey's taxonomy covers the setting ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.486/)), but no survey found treats label-name semantics in classification as a parametric prior. Stating that framing is a contribution in its own right.

Two results explain the two chapter 1 models. **Context-parametric inversion** (ICLR 2025 oral) shows that context reliance under conflict first rises and then falls during instruction finetuning, while benchmark scores keep improving. The cause is training examples whose context agrees with the model's prior ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10796)). Under IT, every training example has definition and name in agreement, so the gradient never rewards reading the definition; 25% retention under a swap is the expected outcome. On the other side, FaithfulRAG reports that prior faithfulness methods **cut unfaithful errors by about 6.65% but added about 6.42% incorrect-match errors by sidelining parametric knowledge** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08938v2)). That is the RAG version of Flip dropping to 0.72 on original labels. The VQA debiasing literature followed the same path: methods that suppress the question-only prior gain on VQA-CP but give up in-distribution accuracy. IntroD was built to recover both, raising CSS from 58.95% to 60.17% on VQA-CP v2 while recovering ID accuracy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01026)).

Methods that report getting both sides are directly portable. KAFT trains on a mixture of factual, counterfactual and irrelevant contexts. On PaLM-540B it reported **about 24x better controllability under conflict while performing similarly on regular contexts**, with most of the gain coming from the counterfactual arm ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.112)). The IT and Flip models are KAFT's two arms trained separately. The chapter 1 mixes are already a partial KAFT, so chapter 2 has to beat them, not merely reproduce them. Context-DPO uses faithful answers as chosen and prior-driven "stubborn" answers as rejected, reporting **35% to 280% faithfulness gains** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15280)). KnowPO names the two failure modes of plain finetuning as contextual ignorance and contextual overinclusion, and handles them with data-ratio adjustment ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03297)). mDPO adds a conditional preference term, preferring the response under the true image over a corrupted one, to stop DPO from learning language-only preferences ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11839)). In this setting the equivalent is preferring the label under the true definitions over the label under swapped definitions. At decoding time, AdaCAD scales the context-versus-no-context contrast by Jensen-Shannon divergence, so it falls back to normal decoding when there is no conflict ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07394v2)). Applied to label scoring with and without definitions, it should leave original-label accuracy intact while correcting swaps. That makes it a cheap, training-free baseline on the existing IT checkpoint.

One methodological warning carries over. Teney et al. showed that many VQA-CP methods exploited knowledge of how the OOD split was built and selected models on the OOD test set ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09241)). Chapter 2 has to select checkpoints on held-out data, not on swapped-label test sets, and report original and swapped performance from the same model.

## Consistency objectives exist for every prompt axis except label renaming

The invariance-training literature is mature, and its effect sizes are modest. The closest papers are below.

The pattern is clear. **Objective-level invariance has been applied to instruction paraphrases, templates, formats, demonstration order and MCQ option order. Label-name invariance has only been attempted through augmentation.** Targeted searches for "label renaming", "label-name invariance" and "KL between label renamings" turned up only calibration, Ground-Truth Labels Matter and order-invariant ICL, none of which implements such a loss ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19148)). On the environment-based side, invariant language modelling adapts IRM games to LMs and stresses that the choice of environments defines what counts as spurious ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.387)). No paper was found that uses prompt templates or label renamings as IRM or V-REx environments. Two 2026 theory papers sit just off the gap. "Names Don't Matter" (ICML 2026) builds a provably renaming-invariant transformer architecture for logic and variable renaming ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23169)). Guan and Bradic give a kernel logistic theory of fresh-symbol classification and predict that token overlap between training and test symbols damages the ideal classifier ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07120)). Neither trains a definition-conditioned LLM classifier. The second implies the evaluation should use a large symbol pool disjoint from training.

Definition-paraphrase robustness is the thinner half of the gap. Instruction-paraphrase sensitivity is well measured: unseen instructions cost over 5 points on average, and the gap does not shrink from 80M to 11B ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11270v2)). Formatting alone moves accuracy by up to 56 to 76 points ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11324v2)). Multi-prompt evaluation shows that model rankings change with the paraphrase ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.52)). But **no benchmark was found that varies class definitions while holding labels fixed**. SGD-X, which paraphrases schema names and descriptions together, is the nearest.

## Speech LLMs show the sensitivity but nobody trains against it

Speech benchmarks document the problem without addressing label surface form. Dynamic-SUPERB found that baselines handle seen tasks but struggle on unseen ones, and attributed this to models not using the semantic content of instructions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09510)). Phase 2 scales to 180 tasks and finds no model good across the board ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05361)). ISA-Bench shows state-of-the-art LALMs are highly sensitive to instruction phrasing. More importantly for chapter 2, **fine-tuning Qwen2-Audio on instruction variants improved compliance but caused catastrophic forgetting** of previously mastered tasks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23558)). Speech-IFEval shows most speech LMs trail their text base LLMs on basic instructions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19037)). AHAMask goes the other way: it removes instructions entirely in favour of learned attention-head masks, motivated by prompt sensitivity ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01787)). It makes a useful conceptual foil, since chapter 2 wants the definition to be the sole carrier of task semantics, not something to bypass.

Language-prior evidence in audio LLMs supports reading the chapter as a grounding problem. Text-only gemma-3-27B reaches 48.3% on MMAU with no audio, 22.6 points above chance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04584)), and models keep about 60 to 72% of full accuracy with audio removed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24401)). A 2026 causal audit, seen only as a search snippet and unverified, reports that a language-prior contrastive decoder fixes 40.7% of Qwen2-Audio hallucinations but gives no gain on Audio Flamingo 3, whose per-class language prior is near zero ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13112)). If that holds, the two chapter 1 backbones should differ in how much IT benefits from name priors, which is worth checking in existing chapter 1 data before designing chapter 2.

On overlap, the only speech-LLM work found that changes label surface form during training is the user's own Interspeech 2025 paper. It covers SALMONN-13B with regular, symbol and per-batch random-label fine-tuning, definitions in the prompt, and unseen SLUE tasks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07731)). Its HTML version contains apparent typos ("33 SLU tasks", "33-class" VoxCeleb) to fix in any thesis citation. The closest external work is **AudioICL-Bench (SLT 2026, NTU)**, which resamples input-label rules per episode to separate task recognition from task learning across five LALMs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11252)). It is evaluation-only, uses demonstrations rather than definitions and has no invariance training, but it must be cited as concurrent work. It is also the most likely source of a future collision: the same group produced Speech-IFEval, DeSTA2.5 and audio-aware decoding, so a training-side follow-up from them would land squarely on chapter 2.

## A class-indexed invariance objective, anchored by CE, is the defensible core

Literature-grounded formulations share one technical point that is easy to miss. Renaming changes which token string denotes each class, so vocabulary distributions differ by construction across views. **Consistency must be computed over a class-indexed distribution**: score each class's label string under view k, normalise over the C classes, and map back to canonical class ids through definition order. For instance x with audio a, definitions D = {d_1, ..., d_C}, and renamings π_k (original, fresh symbols, permuted real names), let p_k(c) = p(π_k(c) | a, D, π_k) normalised over classes. Optionally a second axis applies definition paraphrases D'_m. Candidate objectives, in increasing order of ambition:

**(A) Anchored consistency** (Sun et al., UDA, R-Drop lineage). L = Σ_k CE(p_k, y) + λ Σ_k KL(sg[p̄] ‖ p_k), where p̄ is the mean over views. The literature consistently says to keep CE on every view, since unanchored swarm distillation collapsed on WSC and semi-supervised work warns that pure consistency behaves like pseudo-labelling ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07221)). The choice of teacher matters. Using the original-name view as teacher transfers name priors. The view mean is safer.

**(B) Risk-variance or worst-case penalty** (V-REx, PEARL). Add β Var_k CE(p_k, y), or replace the sum with max_k CE(p_k, y). Each label regime then acts as an environment, which formalises exactly the gap between 0.82 and 0.72. V-REx and IRM are cited from prior knowledge here (unverified) ([V-REx arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00688)). This is the cleanest novelty claim, because no paper uses label renamings as invariance environments.

**(C) Conditional preference** (Context-DPO, mDPO). On permuted episodes, chosen is the definition-consistent label and rejected is the label the name prior implies. A conditional term prefers y given the true D over y given a definition-swapped D. On agreeing episodes no pair exists, so original-label behaviour is untouched by construction. This targets IT's 75% failure mode directly.

**(D) Introspective distillation** (IntroD). Distil one student from the existing IT teacher (strong prior) and Flip teacher (definition-grounded), weighting each example by which teacher fits. Both teachers already exist, so this is cheap.

**(E) Symbol-definition binding term** (Xue et al.). Add an auxiliary loss requiring the model to produce, or retrieve, the definition text given the label string. This strengthens the weak symbol-content binding that MCQ work identifies as the residual failure.

Recommended framing: make (A) plus (B) over a KAFT-style mixture the main method, run (C) and (D) as strong alternatives, and add definition-paraphrase views to (A) as the second axis. Two design choices are open hypotheses, not findings: whether a disjoint fresh-symbol pool is enough to avoid the Guan and Bradic collision effect, and whether the consistency term pulls the original-name view toward ignoring useful semantics. The literature suggests PEFT-only training, since Sun et al. found full fine-tuning on paraphrases hurt by about 8 points, but the chapter 1 setup already uses LoRA.

### Baselines that make the claim falsifiable

The decisive ablation is **the same mixture of views trained with CE only, with no consistency term**. GoLLIE's non-significant masking result shows augmentation can look like invariance training while contributing little, so the paper needs to show the objective adds something beyond the data. Required baselines: the chapter 1 IT, Sym, Flip and mixes; symbol tuning as published; a GoLLIE-style masking mix; the augmentation-only mixture above; training-free AdaCAD over with- and without-definition scoring on the IT checkpoint; context-faithful prompting ("according to the definitions given...") ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11315)); PriDe-style permutation debiasing ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882v4)) and batch calibration; and Context-DPO and IntroD where they are not the main method.

### Evaluations that measure invariance rather than accuracy

Accuracy alone hides instability. An ordinal-classification study found label choices raised prediction-flip rates by 59.5 points while moving accuracy by only 1.3 (abstract-level only) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08869)). The evaluation suite should report original, fresh-symbol and permuted-name macro-F1 from one checkpoint; retention under swap; **cross-renaming agreement** (Fleiss kappa or a PromptSensiScore-style discrepancy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12405))); spread across definition paraphrases using SGD-X schema sensitivity and multi-prompt metrics; seen-task accuracy and a forgetting measure, given ISA-Bench; a no-audio control to separate name priors from audio grounding; and external unseen tasks from Dynamic-SUPERB Phase 2 and AudioICL-Bench. Definition paraphrases should be generated with an LLM, checked for semantic equivalence, and kept disjoint between training and test. Results should cover both Qwen2-Audio and Audio Flamingo 3, because their language priors appear to differ.

### Risks

Five risks stand out. First, **the ceiling problem**. Perfect invariance means original-label accuracy equals fresh-symbol accuracy, so if part of IT's 0.82 comes from the name prior rather than the definitions, a fully invariant model cannot reach it. The chapter must either define success as closing the gap from the Flip and Sym side, or adopt KAFT and AdaCAD-style "invariant under conflict, prior-using under agreement", which is a different and weaker invariance claim. Second, **small effects**. Consistency objectives typically add 0.4 to 4 points on held-out variants, and with four SLU datasets (VoxCeleb, HVB, VoxPopuli, MELD) the unseen-task evaluation is thin, so significance testing and multiple seeds are needed. Third, **incrementality**. Reviewers will read the work against symbol tuning and the user's own 2505.07731. The contribution has to be the objective and the invariance metrics, not the label regimes. Fourth, **forgetting**, documented for exactly this backbone by ISA-Bench. Fifth, **novelty erosion**. The searches were bounded, ACL 2026 and ICLR 2026/2027 OpenReview were not scanned systematically, and an adjacent group is active. Several supporting citations (PA-GRPO, Guda et al., Xue et al., the 2610.13112 audit, many 2026 audio hallucination papers) were checked only at abstract or snippet level. Wei et al. 2303.03846, V-REx, IRM, R-Drop, UDA, RUBi, LMH, VQA-CP, Label Words are Anchors, Adaptive Chameleon, COIECD, JUICE, PH3 and Parenting are cited from prior knowledge without verification in this pass and need checking before they enter the bibliography.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what chapter 2 is about. The question is not whether label-free generalisation helps; symbol tuning and chapter 1 already show that. It is how to resolve a context-versus-prior conflict that knowledge-conflict and VQA researchers have studied for years without ever applying their tools to class names. That framing gives the chapter a mechanism (context-parametric inversion explains IT's 25% retention), a target (KAFT and IntroD's "both"), and a novelty that is concrete and testable: the first objective that treats label renamings as invariance environments, evaluated with agreement metrics and definition-paraphrase spread on speech LLMs.

The most valuable early experiment is cheap and decides the framing. Run AdaCAD-style with-versus-without-definition scoring on the existing IT checkpoint, plus the CE-only mixture of views, before building any new loss. If the training-free contrast already recovers most of the swap retention without hurting original labels, the chapter's contribution shifts to robustness under definition paraphrase and unseen tasks. If the CE-only mixture matches the invariance loss, the GoLLIE warning has come true and the objective needs a sharper form, such as worst-case risk or conditional preference, before it can carry the chapter.
