# Invariance and Consistency Training Across Equivalent Prompts; Instruction and Definition Paraphrase Sensitivity

Scope note: notes for Chapter 2 (speech LLM classifying unseen tasks from label DEFINITIONS; goal is invariance to label surface form, i.e. original words vs random symbols vs permuted real words with fixed definitions, plus robustness to definition paraphrase). Verification status: items marked [verified] had authors/venue/arXiv id confirmed against arXiv, ACL Anthology, OpenReview or conference pages during this session. Items marked [prior knowledge, not re-verified] are cited from memory with their usual arXiv id and should be checked before going into the thesis bibliography.

## Q1. Consistency and invariance training objectives across equivalent prompt views

### Takeaway
There is a well-developed family of objectives that render one instance under several equivalent "views" (prompt templates, instruction paraphrases, option orders, demonstration orders) and penalise disagreement, either via KL/distillation between output distributions (swarm distillation, Sun et al. soft-prompt KL, R-Drop/UDA style), via contrastive alignment of hidden states (CoIN), via pseudo-label consensus (Flip-Flop Consistency), via worst-case/adversarial permutation (PEARL), or via RL rewards for permutation agreement (PA-GRPO). Gains on held-out prompt variants are real but modest (typically +0.4 to +4 accuracy points; larger only in adversarial order settings), and pure-consistency objectives without a supervised anchor can collapse or hurt specific tasks.

### Cited Findings

**Swarm distillation / prompt consistency (Zhou et al., Findings of EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2205.00049) [verified]**
- Authors: Chunting Zhou, Junxian He, Xuezhe Ma, Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick, Graham Neubig. Venue: Findings of EMNLP 2022 (anthology id 2022.findings-emnlp.192). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00049v2); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/new-sigs/2022.findings-emnlp.192/)
- Objective: a pairwise distillation loss that makes the model give consistent predictions across multiple synonymous prompts (P3/T0 templates) for the same unlabeled input; fully unsupervised, needs only unannotated inputs. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00049)
- Results: beats T0 on 9 of 11 datasets across 4 NLP tasks, by up to 10.6 absolute accuracy points. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00049)
- Limitation: notable failure on WSC (about a 10 point drop). [search summary of arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00049); a 2025 follow-up (Flip-Flop Consistency) reports swarm distillation slightly lowers observed cross-prompt agreement in their setup. [Flip-Flop arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14242v1)
- [prior knowledge, not re-verified] Implementation details: the distillation is one-directional between randomly sampled prompt pairs (one prompt's prediction acts as teacher, with stop-gradient), parameter-efficient training (LoRA) is used to limit collapse, and an unsupervised Fleiss-kappa based criterion is used for checkpoint selection. Code: [GitHub](https://github.com/violet-zct/swarm-distillation-zero-shot)

**Soft-prompt KL alignment for instruction paraphrases (Sun, Shaib, Wallace; ICLR 2024 spotlight, arXiv:2306.11270) [verified]**
- [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11270); [ICLR 2024 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18154)
- Loss: L = (1 - λ) L_CE + λ L_KL, with L_KL = (1/(N-1)) Σ_{i≠r} Σ_j KL( ŷ_i^(j) || ŷ_r^(j) ), where ŷ_i^(j) is the softmax over vocabulary at output token position j under paraphrase i, and r is the reference (original) instruction. It aligns output distributions, not hidden states; λ = 0.8 (Appendix A.2). Only prefix/soft-prompt parameters are trained (model frozen); full fine-tuning on paraphrases was worse. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11270v2)
- Training data: GPT-4 generated instruction paraphrases (about 1,000 Alpaca tasks, 986 Flan instances, three paraphrases each); each batch holds one original and its paraphrases. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11270v2)
- Results (Table 7): Alpaca-7B unobserved-instruction accuracy +2.1 (MMLU) and +3.7 (BBL); Flan-T5-3B +0.4 (MMLU) and +1.8 (BBL); observed-instruction accuracy essentially unchanged. Full fine-tuning on paraphrases (FT) hurt Flan-T5-3B by about 8 points on average; PT+KL was the only variant improving unobserved instructions consistently. Representation distance between observed and unobserved instructions shrank after alignment. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11270v2)

**Contrastive Instruction Tuning, CoIN (Yan et al., Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.11138) [verified]**
- Authors: Tianyi Yan, Fei Wang, James Y. Huang, Wenxuan Zhou, Fan Yin, Aram Galstyan, Wenpeng Yin, Muhao Chen. Findings of ACL 2024, pp. 10288 to 10302, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.613. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.613); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11138v2)
- Objective: contrastive loss on hidden representations: pulls together representations of semantically equivalent instruction-instance pairs (positives = paraphrased instruction, same input/output) and pushes apart semantically different ones, added to the standard instruction-tuning CE. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11138v2)
- Results: on PromptBench-style perturbations (character, word, sentence, semantic levels) of unseen instructions, average +2.5% accuracy. Code: github.com/luka-group/CoIN. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.613)
- [prior knowledge, not re-verified] Hard negatives in CoIN are formed by pairing the same instruction with a different output, so the contrastive term does not merely reward ignoring the instruction; the loss is InfoNCE over the last-layer pooled hidden state.

**Flip-Flop Consistency, F2C (Hejabi, Rahmati, Ziabari, Dehghani; ACL 2026 long, arXiv:2510.14242) [verified]**
- [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14242); [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-long.71](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.71/)
- Objective, two terms: (1) Consensus Cross-Entropy, a majority vote across prompt variations produces a hard pseudo-label trained with CE on every variation; (2) a representation alignment loss pulling low-confidence and non-majority predictors toward the high-confidence consensus. Unsupervised. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14242v1)
- Results: 11 datasets, 4 tasks, 4 to 15 prompt variations per dataset; average observed agreement +11.62%, mean F1 +8.94%, cross-format performance variance -3.29%; training on only 5 or 10 variations still yields robustness to held-out formats; out-of-domain transfer mostly positive. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14242v1)

**General consistency regularization (NLP)**
- R-Drop (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2106.14448) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]: two dropout-perturbed forward passes of the same input, loss = CE on both + α · symmetric KL(P1 || P2). Views are model-internal (dropout masks), not input rewrites.
- UDA (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:1904.12848) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]: unlabeled consistency loss KL( p(y|x) with stop-grad || p(y|x̂) ) for x̂ from back-translation / TF-IDF word replacement, plus supervised CE; uses confidence masking and sharpening. Template for "KL between augmented views" with one view as teacher.
- Consistency regularization generally regularizes a model so its output is invariant to a transformation of the input; renaming labels can be cast as such a transformation. [Label Gradient Alignment, arXiv:1902.02336](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.02336)
- Caution from semi-supervised literature: unsupervised consistency behaves like pseudo-labeling and can assign confident labels unrelated to ground truth. [Pseudo-labeling review, arXiv:2408.07221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07221)

**Invariant risk minimization with prompts or text domains as environments**
- IRM (Arjovsky et al., arXiv:1907.02893) and V-REx (Krueger et al., ICML 2021, arXiv:2003.00688) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]: IRMv1 penalty = Σ_e || ∇_{w|w=1} R_e(w · Φ) ||²; V-REx penalty = β · Var_e(R_e), i.e. penalise variance of per-environment risk.
- Invariant Language Modeling, iLM (Peyrard et al., EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2110.08413) [verified]: adapts IRM-games to LMs; environments compete via environment-specific heads updated round-robin; shown to remove structured noise, ignore specific spurious correlations without hurting overall performance, and improve OOD generalization, with negligible compute overhead. The choice of environments defines what is spurious. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.387); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08413)
- I found no paper that uses prompt templates or label renamings as IRM/V-REx environments for instruction tuning (see Gaps).

**Permutation invariance: options and demonstrations**
- PriDe (Zheng et al., "Large Language Models Are Not Robust Multiple Choice Selectors", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.03882) [verified]: selection bias stems mainly from token bias (prior probability mass on option ID tokens A/B/C/D). PriDe is inference-time and label-free: decomposes prediction into an intrinsic distribution over option contents and a prior over option IDs, estimates the prior by permuting option contents on a small fraction (e.g. 5%) of test samples, then divides it out. Evaluated on 20 LLMs, 3 benchmarks; debiasing transfers across domains. [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/17638); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882v4)
- PINE (Wang et al., "Eliminating Position Bias of Language Models: A Mechanistic Approach", arXiv:2407.01100, ICLR 2025 poster) [verified; note an additional NeurIPS 2024 virtual page also exists, venue attribution ambiguous]: training-free; attributes position bias to causal attention (favours distant content) plus RoPE (favours nearby); makes attention bidirectional among segments/documents and reorders segments by attention score rather than prompt order. 8 to 10 point gains on LM-as-a-judge in most cases; costs extra compute. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01100); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28841); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/99263)
- PEARL (Chen et al., "Towards Permutation-Resilient LLMs", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2502.14628) [verified]: distributionally robust optimization over demonstration orderings, as a two-player game: a permutation-proposal network (P-Net) produces hardest permutations via optimal transport solved with entropy-constrained Sinkhorn; the LLM minimises loss on them. P-Net discarded after training (no inference cost). Motivation: a permutation "attack" reaches nearly 80% success on LLaMA-3. Reports up to 40% gains when scaled to many-shot/long-context despite training on fewer shots. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14628); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28005)
- InvICL ("Rethinking invariance in in-context learning", arXiv:2505.04994) [partially verified, authors/venue not checked]: architecture-level order invariance of ICL examples while preserving information non-leakage and context interdependence. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04994)
- Training fixes for MCQ option-order bias: Guda et al. (arXiv:2511.21709) define a permutation-bias metric (stability of per-option confidence across all orderings) and use it as a differentiable, label-free training objective with LoRA; rank-1 LoRA gives about 58% average bias reduction [verified only via abstract](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21709). Permutation-Aware GRPO (ACL 2026, arXiv:2603.21016) builds a group of option permutations per question and rewards cross-permutation consistency; argues static permutation augmentation treats permutations as independent samples and misses the consistency signal [abstract only](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.1621/). Xue et al. (arXiv:2406.01026) show selection bias persists through SFT due to weak binding between option content and symbol labels, and fix it by adding option content to the loss and reweighting symbol vs content terms [abstract only](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01026v2).

### Inferences
- The candidate Chapter 2 loss (render one instance under K label renamings with fixed definitions, add consistency over the class distribution) is structurally closest to Sun et al. (KL to a reference view, weighted λ, soft-prompt or PEFT only) and to PA-GRPO / Guda et al. (permutation group, consistency over options). The key difference: here the view transformation changes which token string denotes each class, so consistency must be computed over a class-indexed distribution (map each view's label-token probabilities back to canonical class ids via the definition order) and not over raw vocabulary distributions, which differ by construction across renamings.
- Design choices supported by the literature: (i) keep a supervised CE anchor on every view (Sun et al., UDA, F2C all anchor; unanchored swarm distillation failed on WSC), (ii) use a reference/teacher view with stop-gradient or a symmetric KL (R-Drop), (iii) consider worst-case over renamings (PEARL / V-REx variance penalty) rather than only average, since the "random symbol" view is likely the hardest and dominates failure, (iv) PEFT-only training was more robust than full fine-tuning in Sun et al.
- A V-REx style penalty with each label-surface condition (original words, symbols, permuted real words) as an environment is a natural, cheap formalisation: Var over environment risks directly targets the gap the thesis wants to close.
- Permuted real words is the adversarial case analogous to flipped labels: the model must override the prior semantics of a word with the definition. MCQ debiasing work (PriDe token bias; Xue et al. weak symbol binding) suggests the main failure is a label-token prior, which a class-level consistency loss plus symbol-content binding term may address.

### Gaps
- Exact per-dataset numbers for CoIN and the precise CoIN loss (temperature, negative construction) were not re-read in full this session.
- No paper found that applies IRM/V-REx with prompts, templates, or label renamings as environments in instruction tuning or ICL.
- Speech-LLM specific consistency training across prompts was not searched (out of scope here).

## Q2. Prompt and instruction sensitivity benchmarks, and the training fixes they propose

### Takeaway
Sensitivity to semantically irrelevant prompt changes is large and persistent: up to 56 to 76 accuracy points from formatting alone (FormatSpread), over 5 points average drop for unseen instruction paraphrases even in instruction-tuned models (Sun et al.), and rankings of models change with template choice (Mizrahi et al.). Critically for Chapter 2, Webson and Pavlick found that model behaviour depends more on the verbalizer (label target words) than on the instruction text. Most benchmark papers propose evaluation protocols rather than training fixes; training fixes come from Sun et al. (soft-prompt KL), CoIN, swarm distillation and F2C.

### Cited Findings
- Webson and Pavlick, "Do Prompt-Based Models Really Understand the Meaning of Their Prompts?", NAACL 2022, pp. 2300 to 2344, arXiv:2109.01247 [verified]: 30+ manually written NLI templates; models learn as fast with irrelevant or pathologically misleading prompts as with good ones, including at 175B and in instruction-tuned models; performance depends more on the choice of LM target words (verbalizer) than on the prompt text. No training fix proposed. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.167); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01247v2)
- Sclar et al., "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design (or: How I learned to start worrying about prompt formatting)", arXiv:2310.11324 [verified; ICLR 2024 venue from prior knowledge, not re-verified]: v1 reports up to 76 accuracy-point spread on LLaMA-2-13B from formatting alone; v2 reports up to 56 points with a median spread of 6.4 points for GPT-3.5 across 320 formats and 53 tasks. FormatSpread samples plausible formats and reports the expected performance interval without weight access (Bayesian-optimisation style search). Format performance only weakly correlates between models; sensitivity persists with larger models, more shots, and instruction tuning. No training fix; recommends reporting ranges. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11324v2); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324v1)
- Sun, Shaib, Wallace (ICLR 2024) [verified]: 319 instructions written by 36 NLP graduate students for 75 tasks (the "319" count is prior knowledge; 36 students and 75 tasks verified); evaluated Flan, Alpaca, T0. Unobserved instructions cost over 5 points on average; per-model drops (observed minus unobserved) Flan-T5-3B 3.1, Alpaca-7B 5.6, T0++ 11B 2.8, Flan-T5-11B 2.2, Alpaca-13B 5.2; worst case Alpaca-7B on BBL multiple-choice 32.1 to 16.4; gap does not shrink from 80M to 11B. Fix: soft-prompt KL alignment (Q1). [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11270v2); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/SunSW24/bibtex)
- Mizrahi, Kaplan, Malkin, Dror, Shahaf, Stanovsky, "State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation", TACL vol. 12, pp. 933 to 949, 2024, arXiv:2401.00595 [verified]: 6.5M instances, 20 LLMs, 39 tasks from 3 benchmarks; different instruction paraphrases change both absolute and relative performance; proposes multi-prompt metrics tailored to use cases (e.g. developer vs downstream user; prior knowledge: MaxP, AvgP, CPS combined performance score). Evaluation fix only. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.52); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595v2)
- ProSA (Zhuo et al., Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2410.12405) [verified; note the ACL PDF header reportedly shows a different venue string, metadata says Findings of EMNLP 2024, anthology id 2024.findings-emnlp.108]: instance-level sensitivity metric PromptSensiScore (PSS), the average discrepancy in responses across semantic variants of the same instruction; larger models are more robust, few-shot examples reduce sensitivity, Llama3-70B-Instruct most robust among those tested; higher decoding confidence correlates with robustness. No training fix. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.108); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12405)
- PromptBench / PromptRobust (Zhu et al., arXiv:2306.04528) [prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]: adversarial prompt attacks at character, word, sentence and semantic levels across many tasks; found word-level attacks most damaging. CoIN uses these perturbation levels for evaluation. [CoIN paper confirms PromptBench as its benchmark](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.613)
- Wei et al., "Larger language models do in-context learning differently" (arXiv:2303.03846) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]: semantically-unrelated-label ICL (SUL-ICL) and flipped-label ICL; small models rely on label semantic priors and cannot override them; instruction tuning strengthens reliance on semantic priors.
- Symbol tuning (Wei et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.08298) [verified]: fine-tunes Flan-PaLM (up to 540B) on in-context examples whose natural-language labels are replaced with arbitrary symbols (about 30k symbols, 22 datasets), so the model must learn the input-label mapping from context; improves unseen ICL tasks when instructions or relevant labels are absent, and restores ability to follow flipped labels lost through instruction tuning. This is augmentation, not a consistency loss. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.61/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08298)
- Label-name bias calibration (Fei et al., "Mitigating Label Biases for In-context Learning", arXiv:2305.19148, ACL 2023 per prior knowledge) [partially verified]: identifies label-name bias (model preference for particular verbalizers) and proposes domain-context calibration at inference, not training. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19148)
- Ordinal classification study (arXiv:2608.08869) [abstract-level only]: natural-language labels and minimal instructions raise label-order prediction-flip rate by 59.5 and 48.8 points while changing accuracy only by +1.3 and -3.7. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08869)

### Inferences
- Webson and Pavlick plus label-name bias work imply that the verbalizer is the single most influential prompt component; a Chapter 2 objective targeting label-surface invariance attacks the largest documented sensitivity source, more so than instruction paraphrase.
- Accuracy-only evaluation hides instability (ordinal study, F2C agreement metric); Chapter 2 should report cross-renaming agreement (e.g. Fleiss kappa or PSS-style discrepancy) alongside accuracy, and spread metrics in the Mizrahi / FormatSpread style over definition paraphrases.

### Gaps
- PromptBench venue (workshop vs journal) and exact numbers not re-verified.
- No benchmark found that specifically varies class definitions (as opposed to instructions) while holding labels fixed.

## Q3. Has anyone applied consistency objectives to LABEL RENAMING (class name vs symbol) specifically?

### Takeaway
I found no published work that trains with an explicit consistency/invariance loss (KL, contrastive, or IRM-style) across label renamings (original words vs arbitrary symbols vs permuted words) for the same instance with fixed definitions. The closest neighbours are symbol tuning (augmentation with symbolic labels, no consistency term), option-permutation consistency training for MCQ (consistency over orderings of fixed content, where option IDs A/B/C/D are effectively renamed), and inference-time label-bias calibration. This appears to be an open gap the thesis can claim, with the caveat that searches were limited.

### Cited Findings
- Symbol tuning replaces labels with arbitrary symbols but trains with standard CE per instance, with no cross-view consistency term. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08298)
- MCQ permutation-consistency objectives (Guda et al. arXiv:2511.21709; PA-GRPO arXiv:2603.21016) enforce consistency when the mapping between option symbols and content changes, which is mathematically the same as relabelling classes with a permuted symbol set; content is fixed and only the symbol-to-content binding changes. [Guda et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21709); [PA-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21016)
- Xue et al. attribute persistent selection bias to weak symbol-content binding and add a content-aware loss term, a direct precedent for a "bind label symbol to its definition" auxiliary loss. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01026v2)
- PriDe's token-bias decomposition (prior over label IDs times content-based intrinsic prediction) is the inference-time analogue and provides a diagnostic for how much a model relies on label-token priors. [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/17638)
- Targeted searches for "label renaming / label-name invariance / KL between label renamings" returned only general consistency-regularization, label-bias calibration (arXiv:2305.19148), Ground-Truth Labels Matter (arXiv:2205.12685), and order-invariant ICL (arXiv:2505.04994), none of which implements this loss. [Mitigating Label Biases](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19148); [Ground-Truth Labels Matter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12685); [InvICL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04994)

### Inferences
- Proposed formulation consistent with the literature: for instance x with definitions D = {d_1..d_C} and renamings π_k (k = 1..K: original, symbols, permuted words), compute p_k(c | x, D, π_k) by mapping label-token probabilities back to canonical class c. Loss = Σ_k CE(p_k, y) + λ Σ_k KL(sg[p̄] || p_k) (UDA/Sun style, p̄ = mean or original-label view as teacher) optionally + β Var_k CE(p_k, y) (V-REx), or a worst-case max_k CE (PEARL-style DRO). Definition paraphrases can be added as a second view axis with the same KL.
- Because permuted-real-word labels conflict with word semantics, the consistency term may pull the original-word view toward ignoring label semantics; anchoring with CE on all views and using the symbol view (not the original view) as neither teacher nor sole target may avoid degrading the original-label condition. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- The mean-teacher choice matters: Sun et al. used the original instruction as reference; F2C used majority vote; swarm distillation used random pairs. For label renaming, a definition-grounded teacher (e.g. original labels) gives the strongest signal but risks transferring label-word priors.

### Gaps
- Searches were limited (about 16 tool calls); a speech-specific or 2026 workshop paper with this exact loss could exist and was not found. Recommend a final Semantic Scholar check for "label permutation consistency" and "verbalizer invariance training" before claiming novelty.
- Guda et al., PA-GRPO, Xue et al. and the ordinal study were verified only at abstract/search-summary level; authors, venues and numbers should be checked.
